Definition of a Project
❖ A project is a non-repetitive achievement.
❖ Projects are temporary in nature. They are not an 
everyday business process.
❖ Project management is to accomplish unique outcomes 
with limited resources under critical time constraints.
❖ A project is a temporary endeavor to create a unique 
product, service, or result. (Project Management Institute PMI Definition)

Project Characteristics
❖ Projects are unique.
❖ Projects are temporary in nature and have a definite 
beginning and ending date.
❖ Projects are completed when the project goals are 
achieved or it’s determined the project is no longer 
viable.
A successful project is one that meets or exceeds the 
expectations of the stakeholders.

Project Environment and Life Cycles
• Project Management guidelines are just good practice but not what should be always applied uniformly to all projects.
• Project environments and life cycles influence how each phase of the Project Development Life Cycle (PDLC) is approached 
and managed.
• Tailoring is necessary because each project is unique; not every process, tool, technique, input, or output identified
• Tailoring should address the competing constraints of scope, schedule, cost, resources, quality, and risk. 
• The importance of each constraint is different for each project, and the project manager tailors the approach for managing 
these constraints based on the project environment, organizational culture, stakeholder needs, and other variables
Project Environment Types
• A project environment refers to the context and external conditions in which a project operates.
• Types:
• Predictive Environment (Stable): The project scope, time, and cost are determined early, and any changes are tightly controlled.
• Example: Building a bridge where detailed blueprints are created beforehand, and the project follows a strict timeline and budget.
• Adaptive Environment (Dynamic): he project scope evolves incrementally, with a focus on collaboration, flexibility, and rapid iteration.
• Example: Software development for a mobile app where requirements may change based on user feedback, and features are delivered in short sprints.
• Hybrid Environment: Combines predictive and adaptive approaches, using elements of both for different parts of the project.
• Example: A marketing campaign where media production (predictive) is planned in advance, but social media strategy (adaptive) evolves based on audience 
engagement.
• During Initiation, assess the environment to determine if it’s stable (predictive) or dynamic (adaptive).
Project 
Life Cycle 
A project management life cycle is a framework 
comprising a set of distinct high-level stages 
required to transform an idea of concept into reality 
in an orderly and efficient manner (APM Body of 
Knowledge 7th Edition)
Project Development Life Cycle (PDLC) is broken down 
into Planning and Execution. The execution phase is 
broken down into execution and monitoring and 
control.
Project Life Cycle Examples
• Life cycles define how the Execution phase is structured.
• Predictive (Waterfall): Each phase is completed before the next begins. Changes are costly and discouraged.
Phases: Initiation → Planning → Execution → Monitoring & Controlling → Closure
• Example: Building a highway. Detailed plans are created, and construction progresses sequentially.
• Iterative: focuses on revisiting and improving work done in previous phases.
Phases: Repeated cycles of development with feedback incorporated after each.
• Example: Developing a new vaccine where trials are conducted, analyzed, and adjusted repeatedly.
Project Life Cycle Examples (Contd.)
• Incremental: Delivers usable increments of the project in successive stages.
Phases: Plan → Develop increment → Deliver → Repeat until completion.
• Example: A streaming service where features like user profiles, video playback, and search are rolled out incrementally..
• Agile: A flexible approach with short cycles called sprints. Collaboration and adaptability are key.
Phases: Concept → Iteration (plan, execute, evaluate) → Release → Feedback → Repeat.
• Example: Creating a game with regular releases and updates based on user feedback..
• Hybrid: Combines predictive and adaptive approaches.
Relation between Environment, Life 
Cycle and PDLC
• Initiation Phase: Determine the environment (predictive or adaptive) and select an appropriate life cycle.
• Example: If the environment is uncertain, choose an agile or hybrid life cycle.
• Planning Phase: Tailor the plan to the chosen life cycle.
• Example: Predictive projects require a detailed Gantt chart, while adaptive projects need a backlog and sprint plans.
• Execution Phase: Execute the project according to the life cycle framework.
• Example: Predictive projects proceed sequentially, while iterative life cycles involve repeated cycles of design, build, and test.
• Monitoring & Controlling Phase: Use the life cycle to establish metrics for performance and risk monitoring.
• Example: Adaptive projects use velocity and burn-down charts, while predictive projects focus on earned value analysis.
• Closure Phase: The environment and life cycle influence how closure is documented and evaluated.
• Example: Predictive projects deliver a final report, while adaptive projects may deliver incremental updates and post-mortems.

on investment; utilities, labor, and personnel costs; and the loss of 
skills and labor from members of the project team who are not

Resource Constrained Project 
Scheduling
 Informally, a resource-constrained project scheduling problem 
(RCPSP) considers resources of limited availability and activities of 
known durations and resource requests, linked by precedence
relations. 
 The problem consists of finding a schedule of minimal duration by 
assigning a start time to each activity such that the precedence 
relations and the resource availabilities are respected.
3
Simple Heuristic Method
Schedule a task that can start right away (in 
terms of precedence) and its required resources 
are within the available resources.
When competing then choose either:
The one with minimum slack
The one with smallest duration
The one with lowest ID

Addresses decision-making in the face of great uncertainty
Provides framework and methodology for rational decision making when the outcomes are uncertain
Decision makers are often confronted with the question whether to take decisions immediately or to first do some testing (at some expense) to reduce the level of uncertainty about the outcome of the decision.

The decision maker needs to choose one of the decision alternatives (either drill or sell)
Nature would then play one of the possible states of nature (either there will be oil or not)
Each combination of an alternative and state of nature results in a payoff (this should be formulated in a payoff table)
This payoff table is used to find an optimal alternative according to a certain criterion.
